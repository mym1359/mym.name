# Reviewer protocol

## Independent synthetic review

1. Use only the synthetic case definitions in `demo/scenarios.mjs`. Prepare two matched groups of cases, with at least one timing issue, one cent reconciliation check, and one case requiring escalation in each group.
2. Ask reviewers to apply the documented rules first with a calculator and worksheet and then with the prototype. Rotate the order and case group between reviewers to reduce practice effects. Use the same rules and evaluation criteria in both modes.
3. Record completion time, correct peak-payment and fee calculations, correct identification of escalation and the first option for review, and whether another reviewer can reconstruct the decision from the saved record. Record errors and ambiguities verbatim after excluding private information.
4. Report raw counts, medians, and any mismatches alongside sample size and review dates. Obtain permission before publicly identifying reviewers or quoting their feedback.

Suggested worksheet columns: participant ID, case ID, order, review method, time in seconds, chosen option, peak payment in USD, extra fee in USD, timing concern, explanation quality, and comments.

## Future institutional evaluation

If an authorized partner participates, agree on the cohort, inclusion criteria, the incumbent servicing approach, and the meaning of a missed installment before testing. Preserve access to the usual hardship programs in each study arm. Record rule overrides and route uncertain cases to staff. Use approved institution controls for privacy, policy, and bias review.

Select one primary measure such as transition to 60 or more days past due among eligible accounts. Evaluate 90-day payment-plan adherence, processing cost, customer costs, complaints, and new fees as secondary outcomes. Document sample size, follow-up, early exits, and possible differences between comparison groups. Keep files and results inside the partner’s approved environment and publish only permitted aggregate findings.

The current evaluation checks synthetic case behavior. Practitioner review and outcomes on real accounts are prospective.
